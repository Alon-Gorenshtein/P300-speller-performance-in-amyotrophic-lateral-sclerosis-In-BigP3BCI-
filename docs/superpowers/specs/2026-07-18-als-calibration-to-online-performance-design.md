# ALS P300 BCI Calibration-to-Online Performance Design

## Decision record

The study will be a secondary, clinical-translational analysis of the public bigP3BCI v1.0.0 archive. It will not claim to study acute GBS, ICU patients, covert consciousness, survival, or ALS disease progression. The aim is to test whether a pre-use calibration EEG recording can estimate the reliability of subsequent online P300-speller use in people with ALS.

This scope is authorized by the user's request to proceed autonomously with the clinical-manuscript pipeline. It is deliberately narrower than a general-neurology claim because the archive contains legacy assistive-communication experiments, not a contemporary clinical cohort.

## Options considered

1. Functional-severity association: model ALSFRS-R against online spelling accuracy across Studies F, L, and N. This is clinically intuitive but cross-sectional, confounded by legacy protocols, underpowered (29 scored study-records), and close to questions posed by the source studies.
2. Reproduce the original dynamic-stopping, color/checkerboard, or dry-electrode comparisons. These are direct replications of the studies already represented in the archive and are not a publishable clinical advance.
3. Calibration-to-online validation in ALS (selected): quantify whether a transparent calibration-only EEG score predicts real feedback-phase spelling accuracy, then test that relationship in a study never used for model development. This targets a deployment decision: whether a calibration visit tells a clinician or AAC team how reliably the user will communicate online.

## Research question and estimand

Among ALS study-records in the bigP3BCI archive with online P300-speller feedback, how well does calibration-phase P300 discriminability predict character-level online spelling accuracy when evaluated in a fully held-out source study?

The primary estimand is the external-study validation performance of a calibration-only prediction model for online correct character selection. The principal reported quantities will be held-out discrimination, calibration slope/intercept, mean absolute error for session-condition accuracy, and cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals. This is an association and validation study, not a causal or prognostic study.

## Data scope

Primary data are Studies F (10 ALS study-records; 30 sessions; ALSFRS-R 1-42), L (11; 11 sessions; ALSFRS-R 0-36), and N (8; 16 sessions; ALSFRS-R 4-46). Each has 16-channel, 256-Hz EEG and online P300-speller train/test recordings. Study B (19 ALS-labelled archive IDs; 60 sessions) lacks numeric ALSFRS-R and will be described separately, not used to estimate severity-adjusted effects.

The analysis unit is a study-scoped participant-session-condition. The archive uses study-scoped identifiers, so cross-study identity cannot be proven. No result will call 29 records unique people or assume that records from different source studies are independent people. Participant-level resampling will occur only within each study-scoped ID; the primary validation leaves one entire study out.

Files will be read directly from the source ZIP through a provenance-checked selective cache. Only Studies F, L, and N and only their required EDF+ files will be materialized. The archive SHA256 and every selected member checksum will be logged before analysis.

## Operational definitions

Calibration epochs are stimulus-locked EEG segments from Train files: -200 to 800 ms around `StimulusBegin` transitions during `PhaseInSequence == 2`, labelled target or non-target from `StimulusType`. EEG will use the 16 channels shared by the clinical studies, 0.5-30 Hz filtering, baseline correction, and predeclared artifact checks.

The primary predictor is calibration P300 discriminability: an inner cross-validated AUC from a regularized logistic model trained only on the calibration epochs of the same session-condition. A predefined secondary predictor is the target-minus-nontarget Pz mean amplitude from 250 to 500 ms. No Test data will contribute to either predictor.

The primary outcome is correct online character selection in Test files. A test selection is valid only when `DisplayResults` indicates feedback, a target can be recovered from the immediately preceding phase-2 trial, a selected target is present in phase 3, and `FakeFeedback` does not override the classifier output. Correctness is equality of recovered target and selected target. The report will state all excluded and unrecoverable trials.

## Analysis plan

The main model will be a binomial regression for correct selections using only the calibration discriminability score. Development uses two studies and validation uses the third; this leave-one-study-out cycle is repeated for F, L, and N. A pooled out-of-study prediction set will be assembled only from predictions generated by models that did not see that record's study.

All patient/session dependence will be addressed with a cluster bootstrap resampling study-scoped participant IDs, retaining their sessions and conditions. Results will be given for each held-out study before any pooled summary. The secondary amplitude model and an exploratory model adding ALSFRS-R will use the same validation splits; ALSFRS-R is not treated as a causal exposure. Study-specific results, leave-one-participant-out sensitivity analyses within each study, and analyses excluding potentially artificial feedback will be reported.

The study will not use eye tracking: it is available in a non-ALS study, not in the clinical ALS cohorts. It will not make demographic fairness claims because age, sex, race, and ethnicity are extensively missing.

## Interpretation rules and venue

The manuscript can state that calibration features were or were not externally associated with online P300-speller performance in these historical ALS BCI cohorts. It cannot state that the model predicts communication success in contemporary ALS care, is suitable for diagnosis, or proves a biological effect of functional severity.

The realistic initial venue is *Journal of Neural Engineering* or *Clinical Neurophysiology*, not *Neurology*, *Annals of Neurology*, or *The Lancet*. A journal upgrade will be reconsidered only if all external-study validations are quantitatively strong and the clinical message remains robust to all sensitivity analyses.

## Acceptance criteria

- Reproducible, selective archive ingestion with immutable provenance.
- Tests verify EDF header parsing, event-to-trial reconstruction, fake-feedback exclusion, and no test-data leakage into calibration scores.
- Every primary result is external to the source study used to fit its model.
- Manuscript, figures, supplement, data dictionary, codebook, and audited AMA references are generated from the final analysis outputs.
- Limitations prominently cover historical cohorts, small clinical sample, protocol heterogeneity, missing demographic data, and unknown cross-study identity overlap.
